ComplianceDORA mapping

How this component supports the Digital Operational Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554). DORA obligations sit with the financial entity; this page lists the evidence the component produces and what you still own.

This is engineering guidance, not legal advice. Have your compliance function confirm the mapping against your ICT risk framework.

DORA areaWhat the component providesYou still own
Art. 6–8 ICT risk framework, identification, asset inventory Every resource is tagged gha-repo, gha-repo-id, managed-by plus your mandatory tags. All infrastructure is code, reviewed and versioned. Register the runner platform as an ICT asset with owner and criticality.
Art. 9 Protection and prevention One VM per job; least privilege with per-repo boundaries; KMS CMK with rotation on logs, queues, tables, parameters, secrets, artifacts, state and MicroVM memory; no NAT, central inspected egress; NO_INGRESS; secrets kept out of Terraform state; SHA-pinned actions and runner; provenance attestations and SBOM; change through reviewed Environments with archived plans. Central firewall rules, SCPs, GitHub organisation policies, approval of changes.
Art. 10 Detection Alarms on DLQ, queue age, Lambda errors, dispatch and sweep errors, reaped and lost VMs, silent sweeper and broker errors, all to one incident topic. Structured JSON audit events for every launch, termination, webhook rejection and token issued. X-Ray tracing. Route the topic to your incident tool; SIEM ingestion of the audit events and CloudTrail.
Art. 11–12 Response, recovery, backup Self-healing sweeper (lost webhooks, dead or idle VMs); SQS retries with DLQ and redrive; DynamoDB point-in-time recovery and deletion protection; versioned state and artifact buckets; documented emergency stop and fallback to GitHub-hosted runners. RTO for a full stack rebuild: one terraform apply (≈ 15 minutes including the image build). AWS Backup plans for the state bucket; your BCP entry for CI/CD.
Art. 13 Learning and evolving Runbook per alarm; reproducible releases make root-cause analysis on exact binaries possible. Post-incident reviews.
Art. 17–19 Incident management and reporting Timestamps, actors, job ids, commit SHAs and VM ids in the audit trail allow classification and reporting timelines. Classification against the RTS criteria and regulator reporting.
Art. 24–25 Resilience testing Drill recipes (just drill-lost-webhooks, VM failure, capacity, broker outage, fallback) on the operations page. Run them on a schedule and keep the testing register.
Art. 28–30 ICT third-party risk Two providers: AWS (Lambda MicroVMs) and GitHub (Actions, App). Exit strategy: workflows fall back to GitHub-hosted runners by changing runs-on; the platform can be removed with terraform destroy. Register both providers in the register of information; contractual clauses; concentration risk assessment.
